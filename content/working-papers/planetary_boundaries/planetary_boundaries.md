##### Abstract

Despite decades of ambitious environmental policy, German consumption has persistently exceeded planetary-boundary thresholds in six of seven impact categories since 1995, with little improvement over time. We combine environmentally extended multi-regional input–output tables with life-cycle impact assessment to develop a consumption-based sustainability indicator across seven impact categories, benchmarked against national planetary-boundary thresholds. Decomposing impacts into scale and impact intensity per euro of production by origin, we find that imports embody more impact per euro than domestic production in every category. A rising import share thus slows aggregate efficiency gains even as intensities fall in both origins, and the growing scale of imports largely offsets them. Had the import share remained at its 1995 level, cumulative impact growth from 1995 to 2022 would have been 12.9 to 96.8 percentage points lower across categories. We show that the same mechanism operates across comparable open, high-income economies.
