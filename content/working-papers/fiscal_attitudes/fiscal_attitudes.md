##### Abstract

Cross-country differences in fiscal attitudes need not reflect deep-rooted national preferences. We show this by bringing the latent political-economy dimension of HANK models to the fore and applying it to a central monetary-union issue: the national fiscal stance. Comparing household-level welfare under alternative budgetary policies reveals variation in “fiscal preferences” within countries and distinct voting majorities across countries. These majorities depend on initial public debt and the underlying shock: following a monetary tightening, the majority supports a balanced-budget policy when debt is low but deficit financing when debt is high. Following a government-spending increase, even low-debt countries support deficit financing.
