##### Summary

The euro has survived a financial crisis, a sovereign debt crisis, a pandemic and an energy crisis. Using a model with heterogeneous households, we ask who gains and who loses from the common currency when such shocks hit. Middle-income households are largely unaffected compared with a world of national currencies; the costs of crises in a monetary union fall mostly on the richest and poorest households, and are shifted between similarly wealthy households across countries. The euro is stable because the middle class has no reason to want it gone.
