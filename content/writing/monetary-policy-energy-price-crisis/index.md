##### Summary

When energy prices exploded after the pandemic and the Russian invasion of Ukraine, inflation in the euro area approached ten percent while the economy was at risk of recession. The ECB kept rates low for a long time. Our counterfactual analysis shows that an earlier tightening would have lowered energy demand, strengthened the euro and brought energy prices and inflation down faster, at the price of a short recession. A capital markets and fiscal union would make it easier for the ECB to deliver on its price stability mandate in the next energy shock.
