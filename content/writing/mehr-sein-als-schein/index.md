##### Summary

Written for the 150th anniversary of the Verein für Socialpolitik, in a series in which economists were asked to show, with a concrete example, what economic research contributes to pressing questions of our time. My example is the most everyday transaction there is: paying for a late-night snack with a printed piece of paper. Money is a promise of future consumption, and it keeps its value only as long as everyone trusts that the promise will be honoured. The essay follows the history of money from cocoa beans and gold coins through the gold standard to the euro, whose banknotes are backed by government bonds, and asks under which conditions people trust a given form of money, when that trust breaks down and inflation emerges, and how central banks should respond to cryptocurrencies with digital forms of public money.
